I'll be honest: there is no concept. I've thought in a lot of directions, they pull against each other, and mostly I'm overwhelmed. This entry is that pile, written down so I can see it.

## Small games, big wishes

I like small games. I love Stardew Valley, mostly because I can play it with my girlfriend. I used to play Sonic. Both look simple and aren't. Stardew took years, made by one amazing creator, and even that is super complex. Jams are full of small 2D and simple 3D games for a reason.

>> Realistically, maybe there is two days of focused work if you are strict, and then obviously you will not hold in your two-day deadline and maybe add one and a half more days of troubleshooting and stuff, and then, yeah, everything will explode anyway.

But here's my actual problem: I don't want to make a small game. I want to work on something like the next Cyberpunk, the next Skyrim, the next Fallout. That's crazy, and of course it's scary.

>> I don't wanna make Pong on steroids.

No particularly fun platformer, no card game. I was never a huge board game fan either. Catan was fun last time, at a birthday party, even though it almost caused a relationship rupture. The year before, Monopoly did. I was laughing about it.

What I'd love to make one day is a role-playing game. A sandbox, something that lives from interactions. That's about the most complex thing you can pick, and usually it takes a lot of people. Should it be world first or systems first? Is the world just window dressing? I don't want to build a theme park. In The Sims I always went for the Renaissance man: exercise, painting, building the house to a ridiculous degree, selling ever more expensive paintings. What draws me is that feeling of complexity and opportunity at once.

None of this is a plan. These are directions I keep coming back to, nothing more. There's a levelling system I have a rough idea for. I like the star constellations in Skyrim, rune-based systems and skill trees. I also like body-based damage like in Fallout. I recently watched a documentary that was crazy good (link follows). I keep going back to a long Elder Scrolls analysis from my master's notes: [A New Type of RPG](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1_y0Iaq3k_Q) and [The Tragedy of Skyrim](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6NXSxkWQp0M).

>> I don't know how to break down a mechanic from a role-playing game that's new. There has to be a lot of scaffolding.

>> I think it's so hard to break it down to create, like, the smallest slice possible for my idea of the big game I want to create.

Then there's the temptation of the hardware. I could focus on making something look cinematic *and* run well, with NVIDIA tech on a 5090. But then I hit the same question again:

>> Damn, then did that count as doing already all that work?

## Where does concept start?

When I tried to write down the basics of what I'd start tomorrow, my brain hit a wall. I picked up a pen. And immediately I wondered whether *that* was allowed. Building, sure, that waits. But concept? When does that start? If I tell an AI how I feel about the jam and which theme I find interesting, and it hands me ideas?

```chat
you: Is it okay to start concepting on pen and paper based on the theme now, or does all of that have to start at 6 in the morning?
ai: Sketching on paper is a grey zone, but it isn't building. Before 06:00: no code, no project, no generated assets, no concrete prompts. Take a photo of the sketch with the time on it.
```

(That exchange happened at 22:09, translated from German.)

>> The barrier from idea to creation is narrower with AI. It can remove, but also introduce friction.

## Push and pull

Between 01:40 and 02:45 I talked it through with ChatGPT. I told it I could use the jam in three ways: start the thing I always wanted to build, test new tech like AI NPCs, or try to win something. It turned those into three strategies:

```timeline
A · founder | Build the first fragment of the dream game. Payoff: something that lasts
B · researcher | Find out what AI characters and NVIDIA tech actually add. Payoff: knowledge
C · competitor | Optimise for the judges and the prizes. Payoff: recognition
```

>> If I didn't care about winning any of the prizes, I would just start and try to finish anything.

Its point was that I'm trying to optimise all three at once, and that the jam is really two games. One is a four-day tournament. The other is a creative career that keeps going for years. It suggested treating the jam as a *proof of experience* rather than a tiny Skyrim, which I liked. The images in my head are a castle, a masked prisoner and an island in the distance. They're images, not a design.

None of these are decisions. They're what my head looked like at 2 AM, rendered by a chatbot. Its last advice was the most useful one: *"at 2:41 a.m., I wouldn't make any more major decisions tonight."*

It boils down to this. We're creative, we want to make things, and the world feels like it's getting more complex faster than we can keep up. Maybe every generation says that. But technology and information really are accelerating. I watch more reels in a week than people used to see in years, and I hear «it's gonna take our jobs» all the time. I swing back and forth on it. Right now I'm more of an optimist.

There's plenty of evidence that new technology doesn't always help people as much as it could, because of exploitation, pollution and the rest. A lot of things have still improved for a lot of people. I hope this goes the same way, for everyone and not just for a few.

>> I sincerely hope this is an opportunity for many people to have fun, to learn, to improve or learn to use AI for game development, and that people will have fun playing.

It's 3 AM. This website was prepared before the jam too, and it's declared as such. I hope that's not cheating. Building starts at 06:00. This is my second game jam, and I hope this one leads to something.

>> Let's see how it goes.
