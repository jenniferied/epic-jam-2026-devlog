I'm Jennifer, I do research at a film university, and this is my second game jam ever. The first one ended with a free VR headset and no game. This one is the **Epic AI MegaJam 2026**: four days, Unreal Engine, AI everywhere.

The jam entries document the work as it happened; later editorial additions are identified below. Three prep entries cover the time *before* the jam: the setup, the night of testing everything, and the push and pull of not having a concept yet. One entry per jam day follows, and a post-mortem closes it. Every entry opens with a recap of what existed before it, because with agents that build in minutes, the line between "testing my tools" and "already making the game" gets thin. I'd rather show it than blur it.

The words are mine, dictated at odd hours. AI helped condense them and pulled the timestamps out of git and the agent logs. You can tell which is which: dictated passages are highlighted.

The little dragon is Funke. It lives in a terminal pane next to my code and guards my scope.

## Play Darkest Night

[Download the game and read the current instructions on itch.io](https://jenniferied.itch.io/darkest-night).

Playing is the best way to experience the atmosphere, music and systems described here. Start a new game and watch the opening cutscene for the story and cinematic direction. The end credits are also part of the documentation: they show development images, the branch history, tools and the people behind the game. This is a rough jam prototype; the game page carries the available download and current notes.

1. Open the itch.io page above and check its platform, requirements and known issues.
2. Download the Windows build, extract the complete archive to a local folder, then launch the game executable from that folder.
3. Choose New Game and follow the on-screen prompts. On keyboard and mouse, use WASD to move, the mouse to look, E to interact and Esc for the pause menu. Follow the game's prompts for further controls.

## What existed before the jam

That preparation included the three prep entries and empty jam-day drafts. Funke, the pixel dragon, was ported from a preparation tool. Agent instructions, MCP setup, prompt libraries, build/test scripts and devlog-capture tooling were also preparation. Jam-day entries document the later game development and identify sourced material separately.
